# Recovering a Team That Keeps Missing Sprint Goals

**Primary Skill:** Facilitation + Root Cause Analysis

> **Portfolio case study:** This is a realistic simulation intended to demonstrate Scrum Master problem-solving. It should not be presented as a claim of specific client/project experience.

## 1. Scenario

A Scrum Team has missed its Sprint Goal for three consecutive Sprints even though estimates appear reasonably consistent.

## 2. Business / Delivery Impact

The team becomes less predictable, stakeholders lose confidence, and Sprint Planning becomes increasingly defensive.

## 3. What I Would Observe

- Frequent carryover
- Unplanned production/support work
- Late dependency discovery
- Oversized stories
- Testing concentrated near Sprint end
- Team optimizing for individual tasks instead of the Sprint Goal

## 4. Root Cause Analysis

Do not assume estimation is the root cause. Facilitate inspection of Sprint Goal achievement, carryover, unplanned work, blocked time, dependencies, and quality signals. Map the recurring causes with the team.

## 5. Scrum Master's Approach

Facilitate a focused retrospective around the Sprint Goal; identify the highest-impact systemic constraint; improve refinement and story slicing; make dependencies visible earlier; agree on explicit capacity for known support work; strengthen collaboration across development and QA.

## 6. Metrics to Inspect

- Sprint Goal achievement rate
- Carryover percentage
- Unplanned work percentage
- Blocked time
- Cycle time
- Escaped defects

## 7. Improvement Experiment

The team would agree on a small, measurable experiment rather than introducing a large process change all at once.

```text
Problem → Hypothesis → Experiment → Measure → Inspect → Adapt
```

## 8. Expected Outcome

Predictability improves, Sprint Goals are achieved more consistently, and the team learns to plan around real capacity instead of optimistic assumptions.

## 9. Scrum Master Learning

The Scrum Master should improve the system around the team rather than simply asking the team to 'work harder' or estimate better.

## 10. Interview Answer

“I would first inspect the pattern behind the missed Sprint Goals rather than blaming estimation. I would use data on carryover, unplanned work, blockers and dependencies, facilitate a root-cause discussion, and help the team run a focused improvement experiment.”

## 12. Portfolio Takeaway

> **The Scrum Master creates the conditions for the team to solve the problem; the Scrum Master does not become the team's problem solver.**
